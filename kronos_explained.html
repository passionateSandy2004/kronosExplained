Step 1

OHLCV → discrete integer IDs

Each candle (Open, High, Low, Close, Volume) is a 5-number tuple. Kronos runs it through a quantizer that buckets continuous values into a finite vocabulary — exactly like BPE for text, except instead of characters you're bucketing price ranges and volumes.

Each candle becomes exactly 2 integers: one coarse ID (rough price direction / magnitude) and one fine ID (the residual detail within that bucket). Nothing else.

Step 2

How the token sequence is laid out

A history of N candles becomes a sequence of 2N tokens. The model sees them in order: coarse₁, fine₁, coarse₂, fine₂ … It's identical to how an LLM processes characters — just market candles instead of words.

Step 3

The model — standard GPT decoder

Literally the same architecture as GPT. Decoder-only transformer. Self-attention + feedforward, stacked N times. No special financial layers. No domain-specific modules. Nothing exotic.

Input tokens go through an embedding lookup, then through stacked attention + FFN layers, then a linear head projects to logits over the vocabulary size (256 coarse IDs or fine IDs).

Step 4

Hierarchical decode — what it actually means

"Hierarchical" sounds fancy. It's this: to generate the next candle, the model does two sequential forward passes (or two sequential output heads in one pass).

1.
Run forward pass → predict coarse token → argmax or sample
C:91
2.
Append C:91 to sequence → run again → predict fine token
F:55
✓
Decode C:91 + F:55 back to OHLCV numbers → that's the predicted next candle

Two softmax outputs. Two cross-entropy losses summed. That's the entire training objective. Nothing else.

The actual edge

Training data scale — this is the whole paper

Architecture: standard. Tokenization: a well-known idea (VQ-VAE applied to time series). The only genuine contribution is the dataset size and financial-domain exclusivity.

Other models trained on generic time series (weather, IoT, traffic) with ≤1% financial data. Kronos trained on 100% financial data across 7 timeframes (1min → weekly). The pattern library it builds — what "a flush into support looks like across 30 countries" — is simply unavailable to any single-market model. That's the moat.

Realistic assessment

What it can and cannot do

What it actually learns

Given this integer sequence, what integer comes next? It's compression and pattern matching over 12B examples. It has no causal model of why markets move. It cannot reason about earnings, RBI policy, or geopolitical risk. It finds statistical regularities in price structure — and at 12B samples, it finds a lot of them.

Works well for
  • → Liquid, regime-stable assets (SPX, USDJPY, BTC/USD)
  • → Volatility estimation for options pricing
  • → Synthetic data generation for backtesting
  • → Zero-shot cross-asset signal when fine-tuning is not feasible
Fails silently on
  • → Regime breaks (2020 COVID crash, 2022 Adani event)
  • → Indian options at expiry — IV crush is structural, not in price sequence
  • → Any signal that requires macro/fundamental context
  • → Low-liquidity instruments with thin candle history

The one-line summary: GARCH knows why volatility clusters. Kronos doesn't. It just saw that it does, 12 billion times, and got very good at predicting that it will again — until it doesn't.
